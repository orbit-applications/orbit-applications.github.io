Market insights

Calgary commercial market notes — November 2026

Short, plain-language reads on what our brokers are seeing in office, industrial and retail. Figures are Meridian research estimates.

OfficeNovember 4, 2026

Downtown office vacancy slips to 26.8% as conversions thin older stock

Downtown Calgary's headline office vacancy fell to an estimated 26.8% in the third quarter, down from 29.4% a year earlier. Roughly a third of that improvement came from older Class B and C buildings being withdrawn for residential conversion rather than from new leasing.

Demand is concentrated at the top of the market. Class A vacancy now sits near 18%, and full-floor options above 15,000 sq ft in the best towers have become noticeably scarce. Sublease space, which peaked at over 1.1 million sq ft, has dropped to an estimated 640,000 sq ft.

Landlords of well-located commodity space are still offering generous inducements — tenant improvement allowances of $45 to $70 per sq ft and four to eight months of free rent on five-year terms remain common.

Key takeaway

Tenants in quality towers with 2028 expiries should open renewal talks now — the best floors are being committed first, and leverage is shifting toward landlords at the top end.

IndustrialNovember 12, 2026

Industrial asking rates hold near $12.40 as new southeast supply lands

City-wide average net asking rents for industrial space held at an estimated $12.40 per sq ft this quarter, essentially flat year over year after three years of steady gains. Vacancy edged up to 4.9% as about 2.3 million sq ft of new construction completed, most of it in the southeast and Balzac corridors.

The split is by building size. Small-bay units under 20,000 sq ft remain tight, with rents for well-located product reaching $14 to $16 per sq ft. Large-format distribution space above 150,000 sq ft is seeing longer marketing times and the first meaningful inducements since 2020.

Sale pricing for owner-user buildings has been firmer, averaging an estimated $235 per sq ft for modern small-bay product.

Key takeaway

Large-space users have their best negotiating window in several years. Small-bay tenants should expect little relief and move early.

RetailNovember 20, 2026

Foot traffic on Calgary's main streets returns to 104% of 2019 levels

Pedestrian counts on Calgary's busiest neighbourhood high streets averaged an estimated 104% of 2019 levels through September, led by 17th Avenue SW and 9th Avenue SE in Inglewood. Downtown Stephen Avenue has recovered more slowly, to roughly 88%, tracking office attendance.

Food, beverage and service tenants are absorbing most available street-front space. Average net asking rents for main-street units under 3,000 sq ft rose to an estimated $33 per sq ft, and good corner units are now leasing in weeks rather than months.

Enclosed-mall and big-box space is a separate story, with stable occupancy but limited rent growth outside the strongest regional centres.

Key takeaway

Street-front retail is a landlord's market again. Operators chasing high-street frontage should be pre-approved and ready to sign quickly.
